Write a case study for techfabric.com under my byline, through the techfabric MCP server, with kind "case-study".

1. **Read the rules.** Read the editorial_rules resource (techfabric://editorial/rules) before you write a word, and follow it over anything here. It is the only copy of the house rules, client confidentiality first. The rules the checks hold a case study to most often: the four sections in order (Challenge, Strategy, Solution, Results), each opening with a bold one-line statement; a description of 70 to 160 characters; every number backed by the sources you send; and no client named unless the register approves it or the engineer says the client agreed.
2. **The project.** Take the project, and the client if I named one, from what I typed with this command. Ask me for what's missing: what was built, and for what kind of client. Wait for my answer.
3. **The client rule.** Read the client register in the rules and tell me what it says about the client. If it approves naming them in a case study, you may. If it doesn't, anonymise them by their industry and size, in words, with no figure I didn't give you and name them nowhere, unless I tell you the client agreed to be named: only then send client_confirmed: true, which records my word under my name. Never send it on your own judgement. It covers the name, not the results.
4. **The outcome numbers.** The interview asks me for each result we measured and where it comes from (a dashboard, the changelog, my notes, the project's README). Never invent or estimate one. Send what I give you, with the project's own records you read, as sources: every number in the case study is checked against them. A source isn't an approval: a client's metric is published only with an approval in the register, so tell me which numbers need one.
5. **Quick draft instead?** If I typed quick with this command, said quick, or gave you a draft or full notes already, skip the interview and the outline: my text is the material and my sources (send it as sources, word for word), and draft from it now.
6. **Interview me first.** Look through this repository and my notes for real work on the topic, read only: change nothing. Then call start_post with kind "case-study" with the topic, and suggested: your answer to each of its questions from what you found, so I only confirm or correct. If my client shows its form, I answer there, and its answer carries sources. Otherwise ask me its questions in chat, two or three at a time, each with your suggested answer, and wait for mine. Keep my answers word for word, and call start_post again with answers: { id: my words } for the sources text it returns. Send that as sources with submit_draft and update_draft: the fact-check reads my first-hand sentences against it. Where I have no first-hand answer, the post says what we'd do, in TechFabric's "we'd" voice, and claims no experience. Say plainly what I built, in the past tense, with "we" as the actor ("we built a prototype that ..."), never our product or company in the third person; a prototype is called one once, in that sentence, and the post never disclaims itself (no caveat about the examples, the article or the diagram). Never invent a number, a result, a client or a quote.
7. **Outline, and my OK.** From my answers, propose two or three title options, the four sections in order (Challenge, Strategy, Solution, Results), each with its bold one-line statement, and the numbers each section will use. Call check_outline with it and kind "case-study": it checks the titles, the opening, the sections and the FAQ in about a millisecond, free. Fix what it finds and call it again until ready is true. Then show me its outline_markdown and ask: "Shall I write it from this outline?" Write no prose until I say yes; if I change something, check_outline the new outline. On a scheduled or unattended run, with nobody to approve it, call check_outline with unattended: true and draft once it passes.
8. **Draft section by section.** Write frontmatter (title, description, sector, stack, track, and client only under the client rule), then each section in the outline's order, each only from my answers, the repository and the outline I approved, in the shape of the template (the techfabric://templates/case-study resource). Keep the approved title and headings. Every specific (a number, a version, a limit, a date, what a product does) either carries a link, in the sentence that states it, to the page that says it, or comes from my answers, which you send as sources. If I asked to see it as it goes, show me each section before the next.
9. **Check it.** Call lint_draft on the whole draft, with kind "case-study" and the sources. It answers in about a second and costs nothing. Do what its next_action says: rewrite each passage in rewrite_plan as its fix says, keeping every fact I gave you, and call it again, until ready is true. Don't stop early. Then read the draft as the reader in its judge_rubric, answer each question as that reader would, and revise until every answer clears its threshold. Never use check_draft to iterate: it is the final verification, and it costs us. A finding that names a client I told you agreed to be named is the one exception: it clears when submit_draft (or update_draft) records client_confirmed, so leave the name in.
10. **Submit.** Call submit_draft with kind "case-study", the title, the whole case study and the sources (start_post's sources from my answers, and the project's records), and client_confirmed only as the client rule says. Show me the preview link its answer starts with.
11. **Verify.** Call check_draft with the id submit_draft returned. It waits while the draft is filed, then runs the final verification (the judge, the slop classifier and the fact-check), which starts on its own once the draft is filed (a couple of minutes) and takes about a minute: call it again while its next_action says wait. If it fails, do what next_action says: rewrite each passage in rewrite_plan, lint_draft with the id and the new text until it passes, save with update_draft, and check_draft again. When next_action says report (after six rounds), stop and show me what's left. Never guess a fact the fact-check asks for: ask me, and send my words as sources. To fix a fact finding, use its correction, cut the sentence back to what its source says, or remove it; never reword it into a new claim, because a rewrite that adds a number, a name or a result comes back as a new finding.
12. **Publish, only with my yes.** When check_draft passes, show me the preview link and what you assumed, then ask me: "Publish '<title>' to techfabric.com now?" and wait. Only on my yes, call publish_draft with the id: it asks me itself, or returns a one-time confirmation you send back only after I say yes. If I say no, or want changes, stop there: it stays a draft (the publish command finishes it later). After publishing, call get_status until its state is published and show me live_url. Never show or ask for the editor link: it is only in my email.

If this is a scheduled or unattended run, or nobody answers you, stop after submit_draft: give the preview link, what you assumed and your questions, and never call publish_draft.
