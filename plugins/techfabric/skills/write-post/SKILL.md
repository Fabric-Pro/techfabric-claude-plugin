Write a blog post for techfabric.com under my byline, through the techfabric MCP server.

1. **Read the rules.** Read the editorial_rules resource (techfabric://editorial/rules) before you write a word, and follow it over anything here. It is the only copy of the house rules, client confidentiality first. The rules the checks hold a post to most often: the reader is a technology or business leader deciding whether this matters to their organisation (the leadership reader), unless the topic names another; the opening pattern, where sentence one is the reader's problem in their words with a concrete detail and sentence two is who we are and our first-hand angle; a title that is one clause naming the subject; a description of 70 to 160 characters in the same order as the opening; a diagram (a mermaid block); and FAQs at the end.
2. **The topic.** Take it from what I typed with this command. If that is "today's brief", call get_brief. If its status is none, tell me its reason and stop: no topic of your own. Otherwise write on its topic and angle, for its target_query, from its sources and linking its internal_links where a reader would want them, and keep its brief_id for submit_draft. If it is a topic of my own, call the server's search tool (search) on the topic to see what techfabric.com already says, so the post adds to it rather than repeating it, and note the pages worth linking. If I typed nothing, ask me what the post is about, or whether to take today's brief, and wait.
3. **Ask for the first-hand part.** Look through this repository and my notes for real work on the topic, read only: change nothing. Then ask me, in one message, for what only I know: what we built or what broke, a number we measured and where it comes from, and what we'd do differently. Wait for my answers, and keep them word for word: send them as sources with submit_draft and update_draft, because the fact-check treats them as my own account of the work, and a first-hand sentence my sources don't back comes back as a finding. Where I have none, write in TechFabric's "we'd" voice and claim no experience. Say plainly what I built, in the past tense, with "we" as the actor ("we built a prototype that ..."), never our product or company in the third person; a prototype is called one once, in that sentence, and the post never disclaims itself (no caveat about the examples, the article or the diagram). Never invent a number, a result, a client or a quote.
4. **Draft.** Write the whole post: YAML frontmatter (title and description at least), an introduction before the first heading, the body, a diagram and the FAQs.
5. **Check it.** Call lint_draft on the whole draft. It answers in about a second and costs nothing. Do what its next_action says: rewrite each passage in rewrite_plan as its fix says, keeping every fact I gave you, and call it again, until ready is true. Don't stop early. Then read the draft as the reader in its judge_rubric, answer each question as that reader would, and revise until every answer clears its threshold. Never use check_draft to iterate: it is the final verification, and it costs us.
6. **Submit.** Call submit_draft with the title, the whole post and my words as sources, and the brief's brief_id if it is from the brief. Its answer starts with the preview link: show it to me.
7. **Verify.** Call check_draft with the id submit_draft returned. It waits while the draft is filed, then runs the final verification (the judge, the slop classifier and the fact-check), five to ten minutes in all: call it again while its next_action says wait. If it fails, do what next_action says: rewrite each passage in rewrite_plan, lint_draft with the id and the new text until it passes, save with update_draft, and check_draft again. When next_action says report (after 6 rounds), stop and show me what's left. Never guess a fact the fact-check asks for: ask me, and send my words as sources.
8. **Publish, only with my yes.** When check_draft passes, show me the preview link and what you assumed, then ask me: "Publish '<title>' to techfabric.com now?" and wait. Only on my yes, call publish_draft with the id: it asks me itself, or returns a one-time confirmation you send back only after I say yes. If I say no, or want changes, stop there: it stays a draft (the publish command finishes it later). After publishing, call get_status until its state is published and show me live_url. Never show or ask for the editor link: it is only in my email.

If this is a scheduled or unattended run, or nobody answers you, stop after submit_draft: give the preview link, what you assumed and your questions, and never call publish_draft.
