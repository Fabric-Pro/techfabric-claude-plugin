Write a blog post for techfabric.com under my byline, through the techfabric MCP server.

1. **Read the rules.** Read the editorial_rules resource (techfabric://editorial/rules) before you write a word, and follow it over anything here. It is the only copy of the house rules, client confidentiality first. The rules the checks hold a post to most often: the reader is a technology or business leader deciding whether this matters to their organisation (the leadership reader), unless the topic names another; the opening pattern, where sentence one is the reader's problem in their words with a concrete detail and sentence two is who we are and our first-hand angle; a title that is one clause naming the subject; a description of 70 to 160 characters in the same order as the opening; a diagram (a mermaid block); and FAQs at the end.
2. **The topic.** Take it from what I typed with this command. If that is "today's brief", call get_brief. If its status is none, tell me its reason and stop: no topic of your own. Otherwise write on its topic and angle, for its target_query, from its sources and linking its internal_links where a reader would want them, and keep its brief_id for submit_draft. If it is a topic of my own, call the server's search tool (search) on the topic to see what techfabric.com already says, so the post adds to it rather than repeating it, and note the pages worth linking. If I typed nothing, ask me what the post is about, or whether to take today's brief, and wait.
3. **Quick draft instead?** If I typed quick with this command, said quick, or gave you a draft or full notes already, skip the interview and the outline: my text is the material and my sources (send it as sources, word for word), and draft from it now.
4. **Interview me first.** Look through this repository and my notes for real work on the topic, read only: change nothing. Then call start_post with the topic, or with brief: true when it is today's brief, and suggested: your answer to each of its questions from what you found, so I only confirm or correct. If my client shows its form, I answer there, and its answer carries sources. Otherwise ask me its questions in chat, two or three at a time, each with your suggested answer, and wait for mine. Keep my answers word for word, and call start_post again with answers: { id: my words } for the sources text it returns. Send that as sources with submit_draft and update_draft: the fact-check reads my first-hand sentences against it. Where I have no first-hand answer, the post says what we'd do, in TechFabric's "we'd" voice, and claims no experience. Say plainly what I built, in the past tense, with "we" as the actor ("we built a prototype that ..."), never our product or company in the third person; a prototype is called one once, in that sentence, and the post never disclaims itself (no caveat about the examples, the article or the diagram). Never invent a number, a result, a client or a quote. On a scheduled or unattended run, call start_post with form: false; with nobody to answer, skip the questions, write in the "we'd" voice and keep your questions for the end.
5. **Outline, and my OK.** From my answers, propose two or three title options, the opening's first two sentences (the reader's problem in their words with one concrete detail, then who we are and our first-hand angle), four to six section headings with one line each, the diagram (what it draws) and three or more FAQ questions. Call check_outline with it: it checks the titles, the opening, the sections and the FAQ in about a millisecond, free. Fix what it finds and call it again until ready is true. Then show me its outline_markdown and ask: "Shall I write it from this outline?" Write no prose until I say yes; if I change something, check_outline the new outline. On a scheduled or unattended run, with nobody to approve it, call check_outline with unattended: true and draft once it passes.
6. **Draft section by section.** Write the frontmatter (title and description at least), the opening from the outline's two sentences, then each section in the outline's order, the diagram, the ending and the FAQs, each only from my answers, the repository and the outline I approved, in the shape of the template (the techfabric://templates/blog-post resource). Keep the approved title and headings. Every specific (a number, a version, a limit, a date, what a product does) either carries a link, in the sentence that states it, to the page that says it, or comes from my answers, which you send as sources. If I asked to see it as it goes, show me each section before the next.
7. **Diagrams and links.** If the server has preview_diagram, call preview_diagram with the whole post, fix each diagram it reports at the line it names, and call it again until every diagram parses and has no finding, three rounds at most: then tell me what's left. If the server has suggest_links_and_topics, call suggest_links_and_topics with the whole post and link the pages it suggests that help the reader, once each, where it says they fit.
8. **Check it.** Call lint_draft on the whole draft. It answers in about a second and costs nothing. Do what its next_action says: rewrite each passage in rewrite_plan as its fix says, keeping every fact I gave you, and call it again, until ready is true. Don't stop early. Then read the draft as the reader in its judge_rubric, answer each question as that reader would, and revise until every answer clears its threshold. Never use check_draft to iterate: it is the final verification, and it costs us. When it passes, or next_action says submit, go straight on to Submit: don't stop after lint, because nothing is on the site until it is filed.
9. **Submit, straight after lint.** File it now with submit_draft (it's private until you publish); the preview opens in the draft app. Call it with the title, the whole post and my words as sources (the sources start_post returned from my answers, word for word), and the brief's brief_id if it is from the brief. Its answer starts with the preview link: show it to me.
10. **Verify.** Call check_draft with the id submit_draft returned. It waits while the draft is filed, then runs the final verification (the judge, the slop classifier and the fact-check), which starts on its own once the draft is filed (a couple of minutes) and takes about a minute: call it again while its next_action says wait. If it fails, do what next_action says: rewrite each passage in rewrite_plan, lint_draft with the id and the new text until it passes, save with update_draft, and check_draft again. When next_action says report (after six rounds), stop and show me what's left. Never guess a fact the fact-check asks for: ask me, and send my words as sources. To fix a fact finding, use its correction, cut the sentence back to what its source says, or remove it; never reword it into a new claim, because a rewrite that adds a number, a name or a result comes back as a new finding.
11. **Publish, only with my yes.** When check_draft passes, show me the preview link and what you assumed, then ask me: "Publish '<title>' to techfabric.com now?" and wait. Only on my yes, call publish_draft with the id: it asks me itself, or returns a one-time confirmation you send back only after I say yes. If I say no, or want changes, stop there: it stays a draft (the publish command finishes it later). After publishing, call get_status until its state is published and show me live_url. Never show or ask for the editor link: it is only in my email.

If this is a scheduled or unattended run, or nobody answers you, stop after submit_draft: give the preview link, what you assumed and your questions, and never call publish_draft.
